## 🔧 Usage

### GUI

- **Login screen** — email, password, _Remember login_, theme, and language.
- **Top bar** — app title, mock-mode indicator, Settings, and Logout.
- **Auto-Vote controls** — Start/Stop, a status badge (running / waiting / idle), the last-run timestamp, and the cycle count for the session.
- **Challenge list** — each card shows the title, end time, your exposure, and voting status. A **⚙️** button opens a per-challenge override modal (any voting setting can be overridden here; unset values fall back to your global defaults).
- **Jump-to-challenge bar** — a list of all active challenges above the cards; click a name to scroll straight to its card. A boost-window strip above it highlights challenges whose boost window is currently open.
- **Challenge details** — your rank/exposure/votes, your submitted photos, and boost/turbo status.
- **Per-entry actions** — on each photo, **🚀 Apply Boost** and **⚡ Apply Turbo** appear when available. Boost and turbo are mutually exclusive on a single photo, so once one is applied neither button shows for that entry.
- **Play Auto-Turbo** — on open challenges with no turbo held, triggers the mini-game to earn turbo (also runs automatically when `autoTurbo` is on).
- **Update dialog** — appears when a new release is available: available → downloading (with progress) → ready to install (or error).

> **Note:** changing settings or moving the GUI window while auto-vote is running **stops** the voting loop (window moves persist the new bounds to settings). Restart auto-vote after you're done.

## ⚙️ How Voting Works

### One voting cycle

A cycle is a single pass over all your active challenges. For each one the app, in order: applies **boost** if it's due, plays/applies **turbo** if eligible, **auto-submits** a photo to an empty entry slot if it's time, and then **votes** up to the target the rules below resolve.

### The exposure rules (which target applies)

Each challenge has an exposure **trigger** ("vote while my exposure is below this") and a vote **target** ("keep voting up to this %"). The first matching rule wins:

1. **Only-boost** (`onlyBoost`) — voting is skipped entirely; the app only applies boost/turbo.
2. **Not started yet** — skipped.
3. **Flash challenge** — always target **100%**.
4. **Vote-only-in-last-minute** (`voteOnlyInLastMinute`) — if set and the challenge is _not_ yet inside its last-minute window, voting is skipped.
5. **Last-minute window** — inside `lastMinuteThreshold` minutes of close, always target **100%** (exposure caps are ignored).
6. **Final window** — if `useFinalWindowExposure` is on and the challenge is within `finalWindowDuration` of close (default 1 hour), use the `finalWindowExposure` trigger and `finalWindowExposureTarget` target.
7. **Normal** — otherwise use the `exposure` trigger and `exposureTarget` target.

For triggers with a separate target, the app votes only when you're below the trigger, then keeps going up to the target. A target of `0` means "stop at the trigger" (target = trigger).

**Vote on new entry** (`voteOnNewEntry`, off by default) changes only the "am I already at target?" test. When a new photo appears in a challenge — added by you on the website, or by auto-submit, emergency submit, or a fresh boost/turbo photo — the app votes once even if your exposure already reads at or above the trigger, up to whichever target the winning rule resolved (100% for flash, last-minute, and scheduled voting; `finalWindowExposureTarget` in the final window; `exposureTarget` otherwise). Adding a photo dilutes exposure immediately, but the reported figure doesn't always catch up on the same poll, so without this the fresh entry can sit unexposed for a cycle or more.

It never unblocks a rule that skips voting: if **only-boost** (step 1), **not-started** (step 2), **vote-only-in-last-minute** (step 4), or **scheduled-fill-only** (`scheduledFillReplaces`, outside its window) is blocking, no vote happens and the trigger is spent. These are the internal rule names the logs print, which is why that last one reads "fill" although the setting is labelled **Scheduled Voting** in the UI. If the vote itself fails, the trigger stays armed and the next cycle retries it.

### Scheduling cadence

Continuous mode rolls a random delay in `[checkFrequencyMin, checkFrequencyMax]` minutes between cycles. As soon as any challenge enters its `lastMinuteThreshold` window, the scheduler switches to a fixed, tighter cadence (`lastMinuteCheckFrequency`, default every 1 minute) until no challenge is in that window, then reverts. (See [`scheduling.md`](scheduling.md) for the per-platform internals — CLI/Android share one engine; the GUI uses the same math.)

### Boost

When `autoBoost` is on, the app applies an available boost to the entry at `boostImageIndex` (1 = first photo, `0` = last; it steps back past an entry that already has Turbo and past the photo pinned for Turbo — a pin blocks Boost while Turbo can still fire, and gives way only once the boost is within `emergencySubmit` seconds of its deadline with that Turbo still not won; with Emergency Submit off it never does. Once it has given way the pin is only a preference: Boost still goes on any other photo (or a fresh one with `boostNewSubmitOnConflict`) and takes the pinned photo only when nothing else can). When no entry can take the boost it is skipped, or goes on a fresh photo with `boostNewSubmitOnConflict`:

- **Timer-based boost** — applied once the boost has `boostTime` seconds or less left on its own timer.
- **Key-unlocked boost** (no timer) — `boostTime` doesn't apply, because there is no timer to count down. It uses its own `keyUnlockedBoostTime` window (default 15 min) measured against the challenge's close time. Since a key-unlocked boost never expires, the default spends it as late as possible for maximum effect.
- **Fresh-photo wait** — a boost spent on a photo the moment it enters the challenge gets few votes, so a due boost is held until the photo it will land on has been in the challenge `boostFreshEntryWait` (default 3 min), then applied on the next cycle, which the scheduler lands on that instant. The time is counted from when the app first saw the photo: its own submit, or the first poll after one you entered yourself. A boost is never held past its own deadline — if the wait would not end a minute before the boost's timer runs out (timer boost) or the challenge closes (key-unlocked), it is applied straight away. `0` turns the wait off.
- **Boost before sleep** — in the desktop app, a boost due within 30 min goes out right away when the computer goes to sleep while auto-vote is running (cutting short the fresh-photo wait), because a sleeping computer cannot reach the API and the boost could be lost. Turn `boostOnSleep` off to keep every boost at its set time.
- **Quit warning** — in the desktop app, quitting or closing the main window while auto-vote runs asks **Keep running / Quit anyway** when a boost is due within 60 min, because a closed app cannot apply it. It is skipped when the quit is not yours to confirm (OS shutdown, an update install, logging out, or a terminal signal).

### Turbo (earn, then apply)

Turbo is a slow-replenishing consumable you earn by playing a mini-game, then spend whenever you like. The two halves are independent settings:

- **Auto-earn (`autoTurbo`, on by default)** — when no turbo is held, the app plays the mini-game each cycle to earn one. (GUI equivalent: the **Play Auto-Turbo** button.)
- **Auto-apply (`useTurbo`, off by default)** — when a turbo is held and the challenge has `turboTime` seconds or less remaining, it's applied to the entry at `turboImageIndex`. It does not wait for an open boost window — boost and turbo only never share an entry.
- **A photo pinned for Boost blocks Turbo** — Turbo steps past it. With no other photo to take it, the turbo is skipped, or goes on a fresh photo with `turboNewSubmitOnConflict`. The pin gives way within `emergencySubmit` seconds of the close if Boost still isn't ready; from then on it is only a preference, so Turbo still goes on any other photo (or a fresh one with `turboNewSubmitOnConflict`) and takes the pinned photo only when nothing else can. With Emergency Submit off the pin never gives way.

Each challenge's turbo can be earned only once. To keep some for a "Win Turbo" mission, turn on **Save Turbos for Missions** (see [Missions](#missions)).

In the GUI you can also apply a held turbo to a specific photo with its **⚡** button, overriding the auto slot. A single photo can be either boosted or turboed, never both.

### Auto-submit missing entries

In GuruShots a **fill** is the currency that tops exposure up to 100% (one of the balances shown under [Bankroll](#bankroll)). Adding photos to empty entry slots is a different thing, and the app calls it **auto-submit**.

When a challenge allows multiple submissions and you've left slots empty, those slots are wasted at close time. With `autoSubmit` on, the scheduler submits **one photo per cycle** following your `autoSubmitSchedule` — a list of steps, each meaning "have at least `count` entries once `seconds` remain before close" (e.g. image 2 at 48h, image 3 at 3h, image 4 at 15min). If a challenge allows fewer images than the schedule covers, the whole schedule shifts toward the end so the last image's time applies to the challenge's final photo — in a 2-image challenge the 2nd photo uses the image-4 time, and in a 3-image challenge images 2/3 use the image-3/4 times. If you're behind schedule (the app started late, or a step exceeds the challenge's whole duration), it catches up one photo per cycle. The spacing matters because GuruShots dilutes votes across entries submitted at the same moment, so staggering gives each new entry independent exposure.

- **`emergencySubmit`** (Emergency Submit) — a safety net: in the final stretch before close it submits photos to any remaining slots even when the normal rules would wait, and overrides the must-include tag filter. Within this same window it also applies any available Boost and any won Turbo even when `autoBoost` / `useTurbo` are off for the challenge, so they aren't wasted at close. Entered as h+m in the GUI (stored as seconds). `0` disables it (which also disables the boost/turbo override); keep it `≤ lastMinuteThreshold` so the fast last-minute cadence is active throughout the window.
- **Tag filters** — `mustIncludeTags` is a hard filter (only photos matching all tags are eligible); `shouldIncludeTags` is a soft preference. `submitWithoutTagMatch` decides what happens when must-include tags are set but nothing matches every tag: submit anyway (default) or leave the slot empty.
- **Chosen photos** — `chosenPhotos` lists photos you want used first, by auto-join and auto-submit alike. Pick them with **Choose photos** (GUI and Android: a picker over your own library, searchable by tag, which also shows why a photo can't enter that challenge) or from the CLI: `list-photos`, then `set-setting chosenPhotos '["<id>","<id>"]' --challenge=<id>`. A chosen photo only gets priority: it must still pass your tag filters and every other rule, and chosen photos are ranked among themselves the way the app ranks its own picks (the visual check reorders within the chosen photos only; the rest top up any remaining slots), unless you turn on **Use Chosen Photos in the order I picked them** (below). A list holds up to 20 photos. A photo can be in only one challenge, so with a list shared across challenges the first challenge filled takes it; a chosen photo that is already entered, or that the challenge refuses, is skipped. When a chosen photo isn't among the themed search results, the app looks it up once in your library (and remembers the answer for a while) rather than every cycle. The photo choosers and `list-photos` do not list photos that have won a challenge, because they can't be entered again, and read further into your library to make up for them; they say how many are hidden. A won photo that is already in a list stays there, marked, until you remove it. A chooser shows your library at once, with its eligibility marks, when it was read for that challenge in the last 5 minutes; the line above the photos says when ("Photos as of 14:30"). After that, or for a challenge whose chooser you haven't opened yet, it shows your photos at once without the marks and reads the library again in the background. **Refresh** reads it again at once — after you upload a photo, say, or when a photo has just won. A photo that auto-fill, auto-join or the Android background service has just entered or swapped, or that you have just entered in another challenge, can still look available in a chooser for up to those 5 minutes, or until you press **Refresh**. **Choose photos to submit** (**Pick…** on a challenge card) always checks first and lets you pick once that check is done. Its **Auto** button ranks your photos by your auto-submit rules (tag filters, Chosen Photos and Submit Only, the semantic score, popularity stats and the local image check) and shows up to five of the best as suggestions; nothing is submitted. Tap suggestions to pick them, mix them with photos you pick by hand, or press **Add suggestions** to fill the free slots with the best ones; with one free slot, it swaps in the top suggestion. It is a ranking under the same rules, not a replay of the next scheduled pick: that pick fills one photo at a time, so the top suggestion can occasionally differ. A search by tag still reads your library.
- **Submit Only Chosen Photos** — `chosenPhotosOnly` (off by default; it does nothing while `chosenPhotos` is empty). Auto-join and auto-submit never pick a photo themselves: a challenge with no usable chosen photo is skipped, and the log says why and what to do. Exceptions, on purpose: the manual **Join** and **+1** / **+N** buttons still pick automatically; **Emergency Submit** ignores it (turn on **Manual Photos Only** for that challenge to keep the slot empty); a paid join that already unlocked (coins spent) still submits an automatic pick on its retry so the coins aren't stranded, unless **Manual Photos Only** is on (then, in Discover, choose the photo for that challenge with **Choose photos** and press **Join**, which tries your chosen photos first); and **swap** ignores both settings, so auto-swap can still replace a chosen entry. With `submitWithoutTagMatch` on (the default), the tag check is relaxed for the chosen photos alone — but only when none of your chosen photos matches the must-include tags; if any of them does, only those are used. So a chosen photo can be entered even when it misses the must-include tags, provided none of the chosen ones fits.
- **Use Chosen Photos in the order I picked them** — `chosenPhotosInOrder` (off by default; it does nothing while `chosenPhotos` is empty). The chosen photos are entered in the order of your list instead of being ranked: the first photo of the list that can be entered goes first, then the next, and the app's own ranking only tops up the slots the list leaves. Neither the ranking, the past-performance lookup nor the visual check touches the chosen photos, a join takes the first usable one without asking the model, and Emergency Submit keeps the first photos of the list when there are fewer free slots than chosen photos. Everything that rules a photo out still applies: one that is already entered, missing from your library, refused by the challenge or filtered out by your tag rules is skipped for the next one in the list. The chooser always numbers the selected photos in the order they will be saved (the number is also on each thumbnail in the settings editors); selecting a photo again after unselecting it puts it last.
- **Where the three apply** — all resolve like the other per-challenge settings: scenario phase, then the challenge's own setting, then a matching [challenge rule](#challenge-rules) (its own value before its profile), then the global default; the first layer that sets a value wins, and a per-challenge empty list beats a global one. The pick-order flag follows the same layers, including a per-challenge setting on a challenge you haven't joined yet. A challenge you haven't joined yet is joined using its own list from the **Discover** panel, then rules and the global default. A [scenario](#scenarios)'s list applies only to fills after joining. Setting Submit Only in the global defaults, a profile or a rule makes it apply to every challenge that matches, and the editor warns about that.
- **Manual Photos Only** — `manualPhotosOnly` (off by default, per challenge only: there is no global value). One switch that stops every automatic photo entry in that challenge, instead of turning off Auto-Submit, Emergency Submit, the four _Boost/Turbo a Freshly Submitted Photo_ options and Auto-Swap one by one. With it on, the app never joins, submits or swaps a photo there by itself, and a [scenario](#scenarios) step that enters or swaps a photo is skipped (a rule with no completed step yet stops there without firing and runs once the switch is off; a rule whose earlier step already went through moves past the skipped step for good). Manual actions still work: **Join** and **+1** / **+N** still pick the photo themselves, while **Pick…** and **Swap** let you choose. Voting, Boost and Turbo on entries you already have (the Emergency Submit window's Boost/Turbo rescue included), key unlock and exposure/mission fill are unaffected. A join you already paid for is not entered either: the log warns once, and you enter it from Discover: choose the photo for that challenge with **Choose photos**, then press **Join**, which tries your chosen photos first. The switch is "on" or "inherit" in a rule, never "off": an explicit off in an early broad rule or a profile would cancel a later, more specific on, so only the challenge's own ⚙️ setting can hold off, and a profile or rule never stores it. Applying a profile keeps the challenge's own value unless the profile itself turns the switch on, which wins even over the challenge's own off. A scenario never sets it: a phase that tries is refused, so that scenario won't import or run. For a challenge you haven't joined yet, or one that comes back under a new id, use a [challenge rule](#challenge-rules) (CLI: `set-setting manualPhotosOnly true --challenge=<id>` also works for an un-joined id).
- **Rule tags** — a [challenge rule](#challenge-rules) can add must/should-include tags; they are merged into the effective tag lists at submit time, so a recurring challenge gets its tags back every rotation.
- **Photo selection** — candidates are gathered with an always-on server-side themed search against GuruShots' own tag index — using your must/should-include tags when set, otherwise keywords from the challenge title — and fall back to your full eligible library if that surfaces nothing. Each candidate is then ranked by an always-on semantic theme score (how well it fits the challenge, `0`–`1`) — with keyword/stem matching against the photo's vision labels as the fallback when semantic data is unavailable — and ties broken by achievement count, total votes, views, then upload date.
- **Open themes and subject words** — before searching, the app reads the challenge's title together with its description. A challenge is an **open theme** when its description says so ("The challenge is an open theme") or when its title names no subject at all — only boilerplate ("Guru of The Week", "My Best Shot") or the challenge's format ("10 Hours", "500 Guru's Picks"). An open theme skips the themed search, the theme score and the visual check, so your photos are ranked by popularity alone (your must/should-include tags still apply). Otherwise a title word the description repeats is taken as the subject and searched first — "Roads to Anywhere" searches "road" before "anywhere" because its description asks for roads. When the description repeats no title word, the title is searched head-noun first: its nouns from last to first, then participles and abstract words such as "fun", so "Balloon Fun" searches "balloon" before "fun". Each fill and join logs how it read the theme, with the start of the description, so a wrong reading can be checked in the log.
- **Visual check** — before a photo is submitted, an on-device image model looks at the top 12 ranked candidates and compares each one with the challenge — its title subject (series prefix, negated words like "No Humans", and your `ignoreTitleWords` removed) and the opening of its description (HTML and the standard rewards text stripped). Photos that clearly don't show the subject move behind the ones that do; among the photos that pass, the ranking above is kept, so popularity still decides. It works on every challenge with no configuration. It never leaves a slot empty: when the title has no visual subject ("Photo of the Day", "Guru of The Week"), when no photo clearly matches (abstract themes like "It's all About Balance"), or when the model can't run, the ranking above is used unchanged. The same check runs for auto-submit, emergency submit, the `+1`/`+N` buttons, fresh-photo boost/turbo, swaps, and auto-join. It takes about 1–1.5 s per challenge on a desktop CPU and longer on a phone; the model loads once, on the first photo submit after launch.
- **Fresh-photo boost/turbo** — with `boostNewSubmit` / `turboNewSubmit` on, the app submits a fresh photo and boosts / turbos that new entry, so an available boost or turbo isn't left unused on an empty slot. The boost waits `boostFreshEntryWait` after the submit (see **Boost**); the turbo goes immediately.
- **Manual buttons** — each card with empty slots shows **`+1`** (submit the best-ranked photo into one slot) and **`+N`** (submit to all remaining slots at once, ignoring the spacing). Manual clicks ignore the `autoSubmit` toggle and are disabled while auto-vote is running.

Newly submitted entries are picked up by the boost and turbo gates on the _next_ cycle automatically.

### Only-boost mode

`onlyBoost` (per-challenge) turns off normal voting for that challenge — the app acts only when a boost or turbo can be applied. Useful for low-priority challenges where you want to spend boosts/turbos but not votes.

### Auto-join challenges

Everything above operates on challenges you've already joined. **Auto-join** (off by default) discovers **open, un-joined** challenges each cycle and joins the ones you want. It runs as a pre-step before voting on every platform (GUI, CLI `start`, Android), and joining a challenge means submitting a photo — auto-join reuses the same photo picker as auto-submit (tags, themed search, semantic ranking, visual check). In the Android app, auto-join, the automatic exposure fills (`autoExposureFill`), mission fills (`missionUseFills`) and scenarios run only in the background service, that is, while auto-vote is on. **Vote All** in the app does not auto-join or spend fills automatically; auto-submit (`autoSubmit`) still runs. A fill that got no answer still counts toward `autoExposureFillMax`, and while the saved count can't be read, no automatic fills are spent. When that happens the log says so: on a computer it names the file (`autoSpends.json` in the app's data folder), and deleting it starts the fill count over; on Android the service tries again every cycle.

- **Scope — which challenges get joined.** Turn on `autoJoin` and it joins **all** open challenges by default; narrow it with the type lists (optional):
    - `autoJoinTypes` — an **include** list of challenge types, comma-separated (e.g. `flash,contest`). Leave it **empty to join all types** (the default).
    - `autoJoinExcludeTypes` — a **deny** list of types to never join (e.g. `flash,exhibition`). Since the default is all, this alone gives "join everything except flash and exhibition".
    - A **rule opt-in** wins over both type lists: a [challenge rule](#challenge-rules) that switches auto-join on, or a profile assigned by a rule that names a title or challenge tag. A profile from a rule keyed only on type / photo count / length does **not** bypass the type lists — it says how to vote, not whether to join.
- **Paid challenges — coin safety.** Paid joins are **off by default** and gated by two caps, both `0 = off`: `autoJoinMaxCoins` (most coins to spend on a single join) and `autoJoinCycleCoinBudget` (total coins the pass may spend in one cycle). **Both must be > 0** to spend any coins. A paid join never charges without a completed join: the entry photo is resolved first (no photo ⇒ skip, no spend), and if the charge succeeds but the submit fails, the state is remembered so a retry finishes the submit instead of paying again.
- **Manual join.** A collapsed **Discover** panel below the challenge list shows open challenges; free ones join on click, paid ones open a confirmation showing the cost and your resulting balance. From the CLI use `discover` to list them and `join <id>` (paid needs `--yes`). Every row also shows the challenge's type (its badge when it has no type), how many photos it takes when that is more than one, and its tags, so challenges can be told apart without opening them; `discover` prints the same. A paid join spends no more than the cost you confirmed: the confirmation passes the cost it showed, and if the price has risen since, nothing is spent and the row says so. On the CLI, `join <id>` prints the re-run line with `--max-coins=<cost>`, and `join <id> --yes --max-coins=N` refuses when the live cost is above N; `--yes` alone pays whatever the cost is at that moment.
- **Upcoming challenges.** Discover also lists challenges that have not started yet, so you can choose their photos early: those starting within `discoverUpcomingHours` (24 by default, up to 168; 0 turns it off and the list is the open challenges alone). An upcoming row says "Starts in …", shows its cost if it is paid, and its **Join** is disabled until it starts; **Choose photos** works as on any row. The list reads itself again when the soonest upcoming challenge starts, and a challenge that has just started but is not in the open list yet stays as "Starting now — it will appear in the open list shortly" for up to ten minutes, re-read every minute, so the row and its chosen photos never vanish in between (its **Join** stays disabled until the open list shows it). The count badge shows open and upcoming apart ("2 open · 1 upcoming"). Choosing photos does not join: the chosen photos are used automatically only if auto-join is on for that challenge (auto-join only ever lists open challenges); otherwise join by hand once it starts. A list saved for an upcoming challenge is kept even though the challenge is not open yet: Discover records the ids of every upcoming challenge it fetched (whatever the window shows), and cleanup of stale settings spares those. A challenge that has just left the upcoming list stays recorded until the open list shows it or ten minutes have passed since its start. Cleanup spares every entry while either list is unknown: after a restart, when the first fetch failed, and when the upcoming list was fetched more than five minutes ago (the CLI may have chosen photos for a newly announced challenge since). On the CLI, `discover` marks them "starts in …" and prints the next steps (`list-photos --challenge=<id>`, then `set-setting chosenPhotos '[…]' --challenge=<id>`); `join <id>` of an upcoming challenge answers that it has not started.
- **Chosen photo.** Each Discover row has **Choose photos**, which saves a list for that challenge alone, and a **Chosen** chip on rows that have one (click it to change the list). A row with no list of its own that still gets one from a rule or your global settings shows "Inherited: N chosen photos"; saving **nothing chosen** in that row's chooser removes the row's own list, so it inherits again. The paid-join confirmation then says your chosen photo is used if it's eligible, otherwise an automatic pick. See [Auto-submit missing entries](#auto-submit-missing-entries) for how chosen photos are ranked and what Submit Only skips.
- **Indicator.** While autovote is running and auto-join is armed (the master is on, or a challenge rule enables it), an **"auto-join on"** badge shows in the header next to the timer — it appears only when the join step will actually run each cycle, not merely when the setting is on.
- **Join timing.** `autoJoinWithinHoursOfEnd` waits until a challenge is that many hours from its end; `autoJoinAfterPercentElapsed` waits until that share of its own length has run (75 = the last quarter), which suits both 24-hour and multi-week challenges. When the percent is above 0 it replaces the hours window. A challenge outside the window is reconsidered every cycle, not skipped for good.
- **Scoping / precedence.** `autoJoin`, the type/coin-cap settings and the join timing are resolved through the [challenge rules](#challenge-rules) (matched against the un-joined challenge itself), then the master default — so a rule can enable joining, loosen or tighten the caps, or change the timing for the challenges it matches, even with the master default off. Only `autoJoinCycleCoinBudget` stays global — a per-cycle total spend cap has no per-rule meaning.

### Missions

GuruShots' main mission rotates through **Join N challenges**, **Use Fill N times**, **Win Turbo N times**, **Use a boost**, **Vote on N photos** and an all-star mission. The app can help with all but the all-star one. Each has its own setting, all global and off by default. While any of them is on, the app reads your missions once per cycle, recognising each by the keyword in its name, whatever the count ("Use 1 fill", "Win 3 turbos"), and counting down as it joins, fills, votes, boosts and wins turbos. The all-star mission can't be automated. Completed missions are claimed by **Auto-Claim Prizes** (`autoClaimPrizes`).

- **Save Turbos for Missions (`missionSaveTurbos`).** Auto-earn normally wins each challenge's turbo the moment it opens, so a "Win Turbo" mission finds none left to win. With this on, an earnable turbo waits until such a mission is active, or until an hour before its `turboTime`. Every turbo is earned and applied as usual, just later. It needs auto-earn (`autoTurbo`) on and relies on a check at least once an hour (the GUI's maximum check interval).
- **Join Early for Missions (`missionJoinEarly`).** During a "Join challenges" or "Win Turbo" mission, auto-join ignores its timing (`autoJoinWithinHoursOfEnd` / `autoJoinAfterPercentElapsed`) until the mission is done, so challenges it would join later anyway are joined now. For a turbo mission this adds turbos to win, since a turbo is only winnable in a joined challenge: it joins only as many as the mission is short of, counting the turbos already waiting in your challenges (including ones still on their timer). A new challenge's turbo can be won once its timer opens. Outside these missions auto-join works as usual. The type, tag and coin filters still apply, and auto-join itself must be on.
- **Use Fills for Missions (`missionUseFills`).** During a "Use Fill" mission, the app spends fills on challenges below 100% exposure, at most one per challenge per cycle, until the mission is done. The fill is spent before the challenge is voted on, while exposure is still below 100% (a vote lifts it and the fill would be refused). It keeps your fill reserve (`currencyReserveFills`). Fills are counted per mission: the app stops once the mission's requirement is met, or when a fill did not move the mission's progress. A mission counts as the kind its wording names first, so "Join 3 challenges and get 2 fills" is a join mission.
- **Use Boosts for Missions (`missionUseBoosts`).** During a "Use a boost" mission, a boost that is already free to apply (its timer is running, or it was unlocked with a key) is applied now instead of waiting for `boostTime`, until the mission is done. It uses only boosts that are already available and never spends a key: a locked boost stays with Auto-Use a Key (`autoKeyUnlock`). It needs Auto-Apply Boost (`autoBoost`) on for the challenge. A boost whose `boostTime` or `keyUnlockedBoostTime` is 0 (off) is not used. With Boost a Freshly Submitted Photo (`boostNewSubmit`) on, the fresh photo is submitted then too, and a boost still held for a fresh entry waits as usual.
- **Vote for Missions (`missionVote`).** During a "Vote on photos" mission, every cycle splits the votes still needed over your challenges (rounded up) and votes that share on each one, without waiting for its exposure threshold. Voting stops at 100% exposure, also during the Final Window Exposure period, so a mission vote can go beyond the final-window target; whatever is left waits for the next cycle at the normal check interval, once exposure has decayed. Flash challenges and ones already at 100% are skipped, a challenge the normal rules already vote on gets no extra, and a single-challenge run (the card's Run button or the CLI `run --challenge`) never does mission votes. A "Vote on N photos in Flash" mission is not driven by this setting: joined flash challenges are always voted to 100%, so it moves once a flash challenge is joined. Only Boost Mode, Vote Only in Last Minute, Voting Pause and Scheduled Voting Only still apply. Rounding up and normal votes can land past the mission's count, which is harmless.

### Challenge rules

GuruShots recycles each challenge under a fresh ID every rotation, so a per-challenge override is lost when the challenge comes back. **Challenge rules** match on what survives a rotation instead. They are managed under **Challenge Rules** in the Settings modal of the GUI and Android app; the CLI applies them but has no editor.

- **Conditions.** A rule can match on any mix of: one or more **titles** (is exactly / starts with / contains, case-insensitive — any listed title is enough), the challenge's own **tag** (Exhibition, Comm, …, not a photo tag), its **type** (default, flash, exhibition, …), its **photo count**, and its **length** — "runs at least" / "runs at most" in hours, measured start to end (24 h = 1 day, 168 h = 7 days) — and its **close time**: one or more end times (`HH:MM`) as the challenge card shows them, in the **Timezone** from Settings, on any date; closing at any listed time is enough. Every filled field must match; an empty field is ignored. A challenge whose start or end time is unknown never matches a length condition, and one whose end time is unknown never matches a close-time condition.
- **What a rule does.** Assign a settings **profile** (any per-challenge setting — voting, boost, turbo, …) and a [**scenario**](#scenarios), switch **auto-join** / **auto-submit** on or off, turn on **Manual Photos Only**, set **Chosen Photos**, **Submit Only Chosen Photos** and **Use Chosen Photos in the order I picked them**, set the **join timing** (percent of the challenge elapsed, or hours before end), and add must/should-include **photo tags**. An empty field means "inherit".
- **Order decides.** Rules are checked top to bottom. For each setting, the first matching rule that sets it wins — a rule's own value before its profile — and anything it leaves empty comes from the next matching rule, then the global default. Only **one profile** applies per challenge: the one from the first matching rule that names a profile. A per-challenge override set in the ⚙️ modal still beats every rule.
- **Default order.** **Sort by default order** puts rules naming a title first, then the rest; within each group the more specific rule goes higher. Roughly: exact titles before starts-with before contains, and more conditions before fewer — an extra condition can lift a starts-with rule level with an exact one, and then the longer title wins. Rules that tie are ordered photo count, then length, then close time, then type, then tag — e.g. `4 photos + ≥ 168 h` above `4 photos` above `≥ 168 h`. Reorder freely with the arrows; saving keeps your order.
- **Broad rules.** A rule without a title can switch auto-join or auto-submit on for every challenge it matches; the editor shows a warning when one does, since a single rule can then spend coins or photos across a whole group of challenges.

### Scenarios

A **scenario** is a plan you write for a challenge that runs over several days — for example "enter one photo each morning, and if one of them takes off, hold it back and boost it on the last day". The app has no built-in tactic: a scenario is made of a few building blocks, and you decide how they combine.

**Where to find them.** GUI and Android app: **Settings → Scenarios**. Start from **New scenario**, pick an example under **Start from a template…** and click **Add copy**, or **Import…** one someone shared. Each saved scenario has **Edit**, **Export**, **Rename** and **Delete**; changes here are saved immediately. The editor has two tabs — a visual **Builder** and the raw **JSON** — and saving checks everything, telling you which field is wrong. The CLI has no editor, but can import, export, inspect and simulate (see [CLI commands](#cli-commands)).

**Assigning one.** A scenario does nothing until a challenge runs it. Pick it in the challenge's ⚙️ settings under **Scenario**, or give it to many challenges at once through a [challenge rule](#challenge-rules) or a profile (e.g. every `exhibition` challenge). From the CLI: `set-setting scenario "<name>" --challenge=<id>`. The challenge card then shows a 🧭 line with the scenario, its current phase, when it next looks, and the last problem, if any.

**How a scenario is built.**

- **Phases.** A scenario has one or more named phases and starts in the one you choose. While a challenge is in a phase, that phase's **settings** (any per-challenge setting — exposure, auto-boost, auto-submit, …) apply to it. They take precedence over everything else, your manual ⚙️ override included, and normal settings return as soon as the phase is left. A phase cannot set `scenario` or `manualPhotosOnly`: a scenario that tries is refused. Nothing is copied into your stored overrides.
- **Rules.** Each phase has an ordered list of rules. On every voting pass the **first rule whose conditions all hold** runs its actions in order. A rule's **Runs** setting decides how often: _every run while it holds_, _only once_, _once each time the phase starts_, or _once a day_.
- **Conditions** — time of day (in the app timezone), time left until the end, time since the start, share of the challenge elapsed, time in this phase, number of entries, free slots, exposure, the challenge's rank and votes, boost / turbo state, your balance of keys / swaps / fills / coins, whether a remembered photo is set, and a test on one entry (its votes, rank, **votes per hour**, **speed ratio** against your other entries, boosted / turbo). Combine them with _all of_, _any of_ and _not_.
- **Which entry.** Actions and entry tests pick an entry by slot, most / fewest votes, best / worst rank, fastest (votes per hour), the boosted or turbo entry, or a photo you remembered earlier — optionally skipping boosted / turbo entries.
- **Actions** — enter a photo (your best eligible one, or a remembered one), swap an entry for another photo (votes, boost and turbo stay with the photo), boost, apply turbo, unlock the boost with a key, fill exposure, vote up to a percentage, remember / forget a photo, go to another phase, and **notify** you with a message.
- **Memory.** A rule can remember a photo under a name (e.g. `held`) and a later rule — even days later, in another phase — can swap it back in or boost it.

Run `scenario-vocabulary` in the CLI for the complete list with every field.

**Safe to leave running.**

- Before each action the challenge is read again, so an action whose target has gone is skipped instead of guessed.
- Progress is saved after every action. After a crash or restart a half-done rule carries on where it stopped and never repeats a spend that already went through. (On Android the background service runs scenarios, as it does the automatic joins and fills, and it writes each note down before it spends. It is part of every normal Android build; only a build without it runs scenarios in the app window, where saving is best effort: the window saves behind the scenes, so a crash right after a spend can lose the note that it was made, and the spend can then be made again.)
- Spending respects your currency reserves and the scenario's own optional **spending limits** (swaps / keys / fills).
- If a scenario's saved progress can't be read, or you edit away the phase or rule it was in the middle of, that challenge **stops** and tells you (a warning on the card and, where notifications are shown — the desktop app and `gurucli start` — a notification). It never restarts on its own. Fix the scenario, or start it over with the Reset button on the challenge card (or `scenario-reset --challenge=<id>` in the CLI); when the progress of a challenge alone can't be read, its own reset clears it, and when none of it can, only a reset of every challenge helps (`scenario-reset --all`, or the same button). A reset lets the rules that already ran run (and spend) again, may spend again an action whose outcome is unknown (no answer, or the app could not record it), and restarts the spend counter. On Android the saved progress can fail to load when the app starts: the card then says the app window could not load the scenario progress yet and nothing is reset, and the app retries (reopen the app if the progress still doesn't load) — that is a failed read, not damaged progress, so do not reset it. The background service is installed on every normal Android build and runs while auto-vote is on. On such a build the card adds that, if the service is running, it runs the plans from its own copy; on a build without the service the app window runs the plans, the card says they are paused, and they stay paused until the progress loads. When the service itself cannot read the saved progress it pauses all the plans and never starts one over from nothing. The app does not show that pause yet. The service retries on its own each cycle, so the pause ends at the next successful read; turning auto-vote off and on again makes the service read the progress again at once; nothing saved is lost. A reset is saved before it takes effect; when it could not be saved, nothing was changed.
- Vote-speed conditions need at least 10 minutes of vote history. Until then they are false.

**Try before it spends.** In the builder, pick a challenge and click **Simulate** for a what-if timeline up to the challenge's end: what would run, when, and why it stops. It works on unsaved edits too. The simulation assumes every step succeeds and that votes and rank stay as they are now. From the CLI, `scenario-dry-run` shows what would happen right now and `scenario-simulate` shows the whole timeline; neither spends anything.

**Templates** to start from: _Exhibition double-dip_, _Evening boost before the last day_, _Morning swap of the weakest entry_ and _Turbo in the top 10_. Add a copy, then edit it.

A minimal scenario in JSON — boost the best-ranked entry between 20:00 and 21:00 once the challenge is 1–2 days from its end:

```json
{
    "name": "Evening boost",
    "version": 1,
    "start": "main",
    "phases": {
        "main": {
            "rules": [
                {
                    "id": "evening-boost",
                    "repeat": "once",
                    "if": [
                        { "type": "dailyWindow", "from": "20:00", "to": "21:00" },
                        { "type": "beforeEnd", "min": "1d", "max": "2d" },
                        { "type": "boostState", "in": ["AVAILABLE", "AVAILABLE_KEY"] }
                    ],
                    "do": [{ "type": "boost", "entry": { "by": "bestRank" } }]
                }
            ]
        }
    }
}
```

Durations are written as `"90m"`, `"6h"`, `"1d 6h"` or seconds. Only import scenario files from people you trust — a scenario can spend your swaps, keys, fills and boosts. The import preview lists every spending action before anything is saved.

**Chosen photos in a scenario.** A phase can set `chosenPhotos` and `chosenPhotosOnly`. The list applies only to fills after the challenge is joined — an un-joined challenge has no scenario state, so the join never sees it. Photo ids belong to one account and may point at private photos, so **Export** (GUI and CLI) leaves `chosenPhotos` out and says so, and the import preview points out both settings. A build older than this feature rejects a scenario whose phase uses these keys, and drops the two settings from its own settings, profiles and rules. Exported scenarios use the current setting names (for example `emergencySubmit`, which an older app version called `emergencyFill`), so an older app version refuses an exported scenario whose phases set a renamed key.

**Notifications.** A `notify` action, and a scenario that stops because it needs you, show a notification on the desktop app and from `gurucli start` (turn off with **Notify from scenarios**). The Android app does not show scenario notifications. Scenarios still run there, including in the background service.

### Bankroll

Your currency balances — **keys / swaps / fills / coins** — show next to the timer in the GUI header (they read `—`, not `0`, if the balance can't be fetched, so a failed read is never mistaken for "empty"). From the CLI, `bankroll` (alias `coins`) prints them.

## 📐 Recommended Setups

**Maximum exposure everywhere** — push every active challenge to the top.
`exposure` 100, `lastMinuteThreshold` 30, check frequency 3 min, `onlyBoost` off, `voteOnlyInLastMinute` off. Start auto-vote and leave it running.

**Conserve votes, strike late** — only vote in the final minutes.
`exposure` 90, `lastMinuteThreshold` 15, `voteOnlyInLastMinute` on. The app waits until a challenge is inside its window, then votes to 100%.

**Boost-only** — spend boosts but no votes (e.g. low-priority challenges).
`onlyBoost` on, `boostTime` 7200 (2h), check frequency 10 min.

**Per-challenge tuning** — set sensible global defaults, then open a challenge's **⚙️** (GUI) or use `set-setting <key> <value> --challenge=<id>` (CLI) to override just the ones that matter for that challenge.

## 📝 Logging

Logs help with troubleshooting and are stored alongside your settings:

- **macOS:** `~/Library/Application Support/gurushots-auto-vote/logs/`
- **Windows:** `%APPDATA%\gurushots-auto-vote\logs\`
- **Linux:** `~/.config/gurushots-auto-vote/logs/`

Files are rotated daily (`<type>-YYYY-MM-DD.log`) and auto-pruned by age and size, on startup and hourly while running:

| File         | Contents                                        | Kept    | Max size |
| ------------ | ----------------------------------------------- | ------- | -------- |
| `errors-*`   | Errors across all categories                    | 30 days | 10 MB    |
| `app-*`      | General application activity                    | 7 days  | 50 MB    |
| `settings-*` | Settings reads/writes                           | 7 days  | 10 MB    |
| `api-*`      | API requests/responses (source/dev builds only) | 1 day   | 20 MB    |

From the CLI, tail any of them with `logs [--error|--api|--settings] [--lines=<n>]`. Credentials are redacted before anything is written to disk.

The visual check logs under the `autoSubmit` category: `Visual check reordered picks for [Challenge …]` when it changed which photo gets submitted, and `Visual check unavailable for [Challenge …]` when the model couldn't run (the tag ranking was used instead). No line means it agreed with the ranking or abstained.

## 🗑️ Where your data is stored / how to erase it

Everything the app keeps lives in one app data folder: settings (including the login token), logs, caches and local state files. The windows' browser session is held in memory only and never written to disk. The per-OS paths are listed under [Logging](#-logging) — the folder is the one that contains `logs/`. The CLI uses the same folder as the desktop app. Running from source uses a separate `-dev` folder next to it (`gurushots-auto-vote-dev`).

**Delete all local data.** In the app, open **Settings → About and data → Danger zone → Delete all local data…**; in the CLI, run `reset-data --yes`. It removes your saved login, settings (`config.json`, `settings.json` and `settings.json.original`), saved profiles, scenarios and their progress, statistics, logs, the state of the Android background service and, for the CLI, the extracted image-model runtime. It cannot be undone. Your GuruShots account is not touched, and the app restarts afterwards and asks you to log in again. It does nothing, and says why, while something is using the data: a voting pass is running (stop auto-vote and wait for the pass to end), a CLI session is running (stop it in its terminal), or the desktop app is open when you run it from the CLI or the web UI (quit the desktop app); then try again. The web UI stops after a wipe: start `pnpm web` again and open the URL it prints.

Without the command, quit the app and delete that whole folder. The Windows portable exe has no uninstaller, so deleting the folder is the only way to remove its data. On Android, clear the app's storage in the system settings or uninstall the app.

**Where settings are stored.** `config.json` is where the app keeps its settings, the login included. `settings.json` sits beside it and is kept up to date alongside it, after every save, in the older format, so older versions of the app opening the same data start signed in with your current settings. The copy goes one way: the app never takes settings back from `settings.json` while it runs, so a change made in an older version is not taken over, and the next save or start writes the current settings over it. The one exception is the login: if you log out or switch account in an older version, the app takes that over at its next save or start, so a logout is not undone. The login is in plain text in both files. The first time the app finds no `config.json`, it imports your settings from `settings.json` once; it keeps the file you had as `settings.json.original` before writing over it, never replaces that copy, and clears the login, the last user name and the chosen-photos account from it, and from every copy it set aside (`config.json.corrupt-<timestamp>`, `settings.json.unimportable-<timestamp>`) that it can read, when you log out. On Android the same data is in app storage, under the keys `gurushots-config` (the settings), `gurushots-settings` (the copy for older versions) and `gurushots-settings_original`.

**Settings import report.** When the import finishes, **Settings imported** lists the profile and scenario references that were kept but still do not apply, so you can create or fix them, each as the rule (by its number and title), profile or challenge that holds it and what is wrong (for example "Rule 3 (Macro…): the profile "Night" doesn't exist"); OK marks it as seen. It stays readable under **Settings → About and data → Settings import** (CLI: `settings-recovery report`, and `settings-recovery acknowledge` to mark it as seen). Restoring settings from `settings.json` does not show it again.

**A damaged or missing `config.json`.** If `config.json` is missing after the import has run, the app restores your settings from `settings.json` and notes it in the log. If it cannot be read as valid settings, the app moves it aside once to `config.json.corrupt-<timestamp>` in the data folder (on Android, into app storage, where you cannot open it) and restores your settings and login from `settings.json`; a notice says so with the path (the CLI prints the same text), and you can delete the damaged copy when you no longer need it. With no usable `settings.json` to restore from (it is missing, or it cannot be imported), the app starts with default settings and shows a notice, however often it is read until you act on it. If `settings.json` exists but cannot be imported, your first save keeps it as `settings.json.unimportable-<timestamp>` beside it (on Android, in app storage) before it writes the current settings to `settings.json`, and the notice says where it went. The notice offers **Re-import file** (not on Android; CLI: `settings-recovery reimport`), which sets the settings in use aside, the login included, and imports everything, the login included, from the file you fixed in place: `settings.json` before your first save, and after it the copy the notice names (`settings.json.unimportable-<timestamp>`; copies from earlier problems are never used). The app remembers that copy until you re-import, start fresh or retry, or for 14 days at most (changing your settings in another run, such as a CLI vote, does not forget it), so `settings-recovery reimport` from the CLI, or a re-import after the app was restarted, takes it too; the notice and the CLI name the file they read; the damaged copy is never replaced by an import you did not ask for. If `config.json` cannot be opened or moved aside, saving settings is turned off and the file is never overwritten; fix the cause (another program holding the file, its permissions) and restart.

**A `settings.json` that cannot be imported.** If there is no `config.json` and `settings.json` cannot be read or converted, saving settings is turned off, the app uses default settings and a notice (the CLI prints the same text) offers **Retry** (CLI: `settings-recovery retry`) once the file is fixed, or **Start fresh** (CLI: `settings-recovery start-fresh`), which begins with default settings and keeps a copy of the file beside it, as `settings.json.original` or, when that name is taken, as `settings.json.unimportable-<timestamp>`, and says where, or that there was no file to keep; if the settings were restored in the meantime it says so and resets nothing. Nothing is overwritten until one of them is done.

**A `settings.json` that cannot be read just now.** If the file is busy (another program holds it) or a read or write fails once, the notice says so: when `config.json` was damaged it names where the damaged copy went, saying that `settings.json`, the copy to restore your settings from, could not be read just now, that nothing was overwritten, and that the app tries again by itself (CLI: the next run does; or run `settings-recovery retry`). Only **Retry** is offered while this may pass; **Start fresh** appears only for a file that cannot be imported at all. If every try for several minutes fails, the app treats the file as unusable, as for a damaged one, and carries on with default settings. A damaged copy kept beside the settings (`config.json.corrupt-<timestamp>`, `settings.json.unimportable-<timestamp>`, `settings.json.original`) keeps the login it held until you next log out; a logout then takes the token and API headers out of every copy, and a copy that could not be rewritten is cleared at the next start or save. Copies set aside more than two weeks ago lose the login at the start whether or not you logged out, and keep their settings. Once a logout has taken the login out of the copies, the notice stops saying they hold it. **Start fresh** over a `settings.json` that could not be read leaves it as it is, says so, and sets it aside as a copy before anything is written over it, as soon as it can be read. If you log out while this notice is showing, the logout is recorded and takes effect as soon as the settings load, in whichever run that is (the CLI says so; if it could not be recorded, the app and the CLI say you are still logged in). From the CLI, `settings-recovery start-fresh` is refused while the file may still be readable again; add `--force` to start with default settings anyway.

**Third-party licenses.** **Settings → About and data → Third-party licenses** (CLI: `licenses`) lists the open-source software and data the app includes, with their licenses.

**Chosen photos belong to one account.** The app records which account saved a Chosen Photos list (`chosenPhotosMemberId`, a GuruShots member id, which stays in your settings after you log out). When a different account signs in on the same device, both Chosen Photos settings are ignored (the log says so once), and thumbnails of the other account's photos are never shown. Once the chooser has listed your library (which tells the app which account is signed in), a list saved under another account appears only as a count, and the chooser starts with nothing chosen; until then the settings editors cannot tell and still show the list's photo ids as text. Nothing is deleted for you: saving a new list under the new account makes the lists saved under the other account apply to it too, so remove them first — the chooser's notice has a button for it (behind a confirmation), and the CLI has `clear-chosen-photos`. Both remove only the `chosenPhotos` lists, in every setting, profile, rule and scenario, and forget the recorded account; **Submit Only Chosen Photos** stays as it is. Scenario exports leave the list out for the same reason.

Logging out removes the login token from your settings; the other settings, logs and caches stay. The copies the app keeps of your settings files (see Where settings are stored, above) lose the token and also the last user name and the chosen-photos account, which stay in your settings and in the current `settings.json` so that the login form is prefilled. The exception is what the app keeps only in memory, such as the copy of your photo library the choosers use: it is never written to disk, and is dropped when you log out or switch accounts.

## 🔍 Troubleshooting

**"No authentication token found" / "Token expired"** — log in again from the login screen (CLI: run `login`). Tokens are tied to your account; if it keeps happening, check your system clock.

**"Network Error"** — check your connection and firewall, raise `apiTimeout` (try 60–120 s), and try again later; GuruShots may be briefly unavailable.

**"API Rate Limit Exceeded" / "Too Many Requests"** — stop **all** instances (GUI and CLI), wait 5–10 minutes, and make sure only one is running.

**Auto-vote runs but nothing happens** — confirm you have active challenges, that your exposure isn't already at the trigger (default 100%), and that `voteOnlyInLastMinute` isn't on while challenges are still outside their last-minute window. Check the logs for the per-challenge skip reason.

**Auto-submit picked an off-theme photo** — the visual check only chooses among the top 12 candidates the tag search found, so if none of them shows the subject it can't help: add `mustIncludeTags`/`shouldIncludeTags` for that challenge (or a [challenge rule](#challenge-rules)) so better candidates reach the shortlist. A `Visual check unavailable` log line means the image model failed to load or run; the CLI unpacks its model on first use (see [Install per platform](../README.md#install-per-platform)), so check free disk space.

**A boost was missed while the computer was asleep** — a sleeping computer cannot reach GuruShots, so a boost that falls due during sleep waits until the computer wakes, and by then its window may have closed. In the desktop app, when **Boost Before Sleep** (`boostOnSleep`) is on, a boost due within 30 min is sent as the computer goes to sleep, and quitting while one is due asks first. Both are best effort: the sleep notice races the network going down, and nothing can be done if the computer was already asleep before the boost became due. To be safe near deadlines, keep the computer awake, raise `boostTime` so the boost goes out earlier, or run the CLI on an always-on machine. In the logs, look for `Device is going to sleep — trying to boost …` (sent early), `boost not confirmed before sleep` (sent, reply lost — the next cycle after wake shows whether it landed), `Boost Before Sleep is off for …` (left for its set time) and `Voting cycle ran … later than scheduled` (the app was suspended or throttled, and whatever fell due in that gap did not happen).

**Window opens off-screen** — restart the app; from the CLI run `reset-windows`.

**Launching the app appears to do nothing (no window)** — a stale or frozen instance may still be running; a second launch defers to it and exits. Quit or kill the old process (Activity Monitor / `pkill -f GuruShotsAutoVote`) and launch again.

**Android: voting stops in the background** — set the app's battery usage to **Unrestricted** and whitelist it in your vendor's battery manager; on Android 12 grant the exact-alarm permission for the 1-minute last-minute cadence.

If you're still stuck, check the logs and [open an issue](https://github.com/isthisgitlab/gurushots-auto-vote/issues) with your version, OS, a description, reproduction steps, and relevant (credential-free) log excerpts.
